The Verdict

Your legal fear was unfounded, and your traction verdict was premature — because the product never actually delivered its core promise. The live UX session revealed that every result table shows anonymous rows: player names come back null for every query, including your own curated examples. Visitors got spreadsheets, not stats. "No traction" was never a fair market test.

Recommended path: Portfolio Flagship with a cheap market option (Approach 2 below). Spend the month making it secure, honest, and demo-worthy — then let the 2026 season opener in September be your real decision gate, with share links and named results finally in place.

Your two questions, answered

No — the fear was misplaced

"Am I right that I can't monetize this without legal retribution?"

The $7,500/mo quote was for official real-time data feeds (Sportradar/Genius Sports — built for sportsbooks). Historical factual stats cost $0 and are constitutionally protected for commercial use: Feist (facts aren't copyrightable), CBC v. MLBAM 2007 (for-profit fantasy products on player names + stats are First Amendment protected, cert. denied), Daniels v. FanDuel 2018 (even paywalled). PFF, Sports Reference, PlayerProfiler, StatMuse, and the nflfastR co-author's own rbsdm.com all monetize without league licenses. Zero have been sued by the NFL over stats. Real enforcement targets logos, trademarks, and footage — keep those out and you're clear.

Yes, but modestly — and the test was never run

"Is this monetizable? Was I wrong to abandon it?"

Legally green, commercially yellow. Realistic solo-dev ceiling: $0–500/mo without an audience, $1–3k/mo over 1–2 seasons with a distribution engine. The market tailwind is real (fantasy ≈ $42B, sports analytics ≈ 20% CAGR), but PFR/Stathead at $8/mo is brutal competition. The honest reframe: you weren't wrong to stop investing — you were wrong about why. The binding constraint is distribution, not lawyers, and your StatNug share-pages idea was always the distribution mechanism, not a side feature. It was never built (see below).

What the four audits found

Live UX audit 2 blockers core mechanic works

The chunk-pill visualization, example searches (first result in 2 clicks), mobile layout, and plumbing (zero console errors) are genuinely good. But the first-minute experience fails three ways:

  • P1 — Anonymous results. player_display_name and recent_team are null on every row; the only identity field that works is player_name = "P.Mahomes" — exact, abbreviated, unguessable. Whose 159-yard game? Unanswerable.
  • P2 — Typing a player's name never works. "Derrick" suggests CollegeConferences. Getting one named stat took the auditor 13 actions plus insider knowledge; the natural path dead-ends in 30 seconds, silently.
  • P3 — The share loop is zero. URL never changes, no permalink, no copy/share button anywhere. The entire StatNug thesis is unimplemented.

Plus: Search secretly requires two clicks with a column-picker modal in between; raw schema leaks everywhere (wopr, dakota, default filter value literally "something"). Full ranked list + screenshots in the appendix.

Technical audit 2 critical core is healthy

  • Critical: /api/query is an unauthenticated open Cypher proxy to prod Aura — anyone with the URL can dump the DB or burn compute. The claimed read-only validation doesn't exist; only the driver's access mode protects you. (~20-line fix.)
  • Critical: the Stripe pipeline never worked end-to-end — metadata set on the Checkout Session but read from the Subscription, so every webhook 400s and is_pro can never be true. And isPro gates nothing in the UI: the ~2,200-line monetization stack is broken and unused.
  • High: ingestion hardcodes season 2025 in four scripts — in September 2026 the cron goes green while loading nothing. (season_config.py already exists; just unused.)
  • High: UI tests can't run (stale mock of the gitignored generated-chunks file); service has zero tests.
  • Verdict: the product core (chunk chain → Cypher → results, ~250-line service) is compact and well-structured. The liabilities are the things bolted around it — and they're mostly deletions.

NLP architecture (design doc) weekend-shippable

Recommendation: English → ChunkChain, never English → Cypher. Your Chunks system is already a closed, validated intermediate representation — the LLM emits only chunk IDs + slot fills from an enum'd catalog, so hallucinated schema becomes unrepresentable; your existing compiler emits the Cypher deterministically (1,167 stays 1,167); and the plain-English chain renders as an editable confirmation before anything runs — which is the structural fix for "meanest quarterback."

  • Cost: ~$0.0025/query (Haiku 4.5, cached) to $0.007 (Sonnet 4.6) — $2.50–7 per 1,000 queries. Latency 1–2.5s, one API call, no vector DB.
  • Phase 1 is a weekend because the validator, compiler, and chunk UI already exist. Out-of-scope questions get a structured unanswerable/clarify response — refusal is a first-class output, not a hope.
  • Honest ceiling: the chunk IR can't express aggregation or "top 5" yet — growing the IR (aggregate + order/limit chunks) is the real Phase 3 work, and it benefits manual users equally.

This also retroactively validates your Part 3 pivot: the deterministic IR you built to replace the LLM turns out to be exactly what makes an LLM trustworthy in 2026.

Why Approach 2 is the recommendation

Your time budget was contingent on the economics, and the economics say: no legal barrier, modest revenue ceiling, distribution is everything. That argues against betting 100 hours on revenue (Approach 3's payoff is uncertain) but equally against pure mothballing (Approach 1 wastes the discovery that the product never got a fair test, and wastes a strong consulting story sitting in your repo). Approach 2 captures the certain value — security, longevity, a demo-quality flagship, and a Part 4 blog post that writes itself — while keeping the September relaunch option open at one week's notice. If the NL demo and named results reignite your enthusiasm in week 3, escalate to Approach 3 with six weeks still to spare before the season.
